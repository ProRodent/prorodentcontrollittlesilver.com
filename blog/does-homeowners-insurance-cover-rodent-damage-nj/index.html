Usually not, at least for the rodent damage itself. Many standard homeowners policies list vermin, rodents and insects as exclusions, and insurers often treat chewing and nesting as a maintenance problem rather than a sudden accident. Some policies treat a resulting loss differently, such as a fire that starts from chewed wiring or water damage from a gnawed supply line. We are a pest control service, not an insurer, so only your policy language and your agent can say what yours pays.

What does a standard policy say about rodents?

Policy forms vary by company and by state filing, so there is no single answer. Still, a common pattern shows up. The policy lists covered perils, then a section of exclusions, and rodents or vermin often land in the exclusions next to wear and tear, rot and neglect.

The reasoning is that infestations build slowly and are seen as preventable. A mouse that has nested in insulation for a season is not the sudden, accidental event insurance is built for. That is why a claim for chewed insulation, soiled attic floors or gnawed boxes is often denied.

Do not stop at the exclusions page. Look for wording about ensuing loss, which is damage that follows from an excluded cause. Some policies exclude the rodent damage but still pay for a covered result. Others exclude both. You will only know by reading your own form.

Which losses might be treated differently?

The gray area is damage that comes after the rodent does its work. The table below shows how the question tends to be framed. It is not a coverage ruling, and your policy may say something else.

Why do older shore homes carry extra risk?

Many homes in the borough date from the mid-century decades, so attic wiring in the older ones has been in place a long time. Aging wire insulation can turn brittle, brittle insulation is easier for rodents to strip, and damaged insulation on wiring can contribute to a fire. If you hear scratching overhead in an older house, have a qualified electrician look at the attic runs.

Coastal humidity adds a second problem. Damp attics and crawl spaces hold nests together and soak insulation, so a small leak from a chewed line can spread before anyone notices. Check the low spots and the areas along exterior walls first, and note any stains with a date.

What should you do first if you find rodent damage?

Slow down and collect proof before you clean up. Your future self, and an adjuster, will want dated records, and you cannot photograph damage you have already thrown away. Handle droppings with care, and follow safe cleanup steps for mouse droppings once you have documented everything.

Document the damage before you touch it.

  • Take wide photos of each area, then close-ups of chewed wires, pipes, insulation and droppings, with something for scale.
  • Write down the date you first noticed noises, droppings or damage, and keep a running dated log.
  • Save receipts for any repairs, cleanup supplies and pest work, and keep any written report you receive.
  • Shut off the water at the valve if a line is chewed, and stay out of the area if you see exposed or sparking wires.
  • Call your insurance agent or the number on your policy and ask before you start large repairs.

What can a pest report do, and what can't it do?

A written inspection report can back up your timeline. It can describe the species, the entry points, the signs of activity and the areas affected, and it carries a date. That helps an adjuster understand what was found and when.

It cannot make a policy cover something it excludes. It also cannot tell your insurer how to rule, and we would never promise that a report changes the outcome of a claim. Think of it as one more piece of documentation, not a promise of payment. A rodent inspection is worth doing for its own sake, because it shows where the animals are getting in.

How do you talk to your agent about a claim?

Ask direct questions and write down the answers with the date and the name of the person. Vague conversations are hard to rely on later, so ask for key answers in writing whenever the company will provide them.

  • Ask whether your policy excludes vermin or rodents, and ask for the exact wording in writing.
  • Ask how the policy treats ensuing loss, such as fire or water damage that follows from an excluded cause.
  • Ask whether a small claim could affect your renewal or premium, since some homeowners decide to pay out of pocket for minor repairs.
  • Ask whether an endorsement or add-on exists for pest damage, and what it includes and leaves out.
  • Ask what proof the company wants, so you gather it once.

What should you fix so it does not happen again?

Because insurers treat infestations as preventable, the strongest move is stopping the next one. A technician finds the gaps, and rodent exclusion work closes them with metal, mesh and sealant rather than foam alone. Trapping clears the animals already inside, which is the job of rodent trapping and removal.

If wiring, insulation or a supply line was chewed, rodent damage repair and the right licensed trade come next. For plumbing or electrical work, use a qualified tradesperson and ask for their NJ license number. Keep every invoice with your claim records.

Common questions

Will my homeowners policy pay for rodent removal?

Most standard policies do not, because pest control is treated as home maintenance. Some have a limited endorsement, so ask your agent. We cannot say what your particular policy covers.

If mice chew a wire and cause a fire, is the fire covered?

It may be reviewed differently from the chewing itself, since fire is a common covered peril. Whether the rodent exclusion still applies depends on your exact wording. Read the ensuing loss language and ask your agent to explain it in writing.

Should I get a pest inspection report before I file a claim?

A dated report can support your timeline and describe what was found. It will not force coverage, and it should not delay urgent safety steps. Call your agent early and keep the report with your photos.

Does filing a small claim raise my premium?

It can, depending on your insurer and your claims history, and we cannot predict yours. Ask your agent about the effect before you file for minor damage.

Do you respond at night or on weekends if I hear gnawing?

Yes, our 24/7 line is open for urgent situations, and we schedule same-day visits where the day allows. If you smell burning or see sparks, call 911 and your electric utility first, then call us.

Are you giving legal or insurance advice here?

No. We are a pest control service, not lawyers or insurers, and this article is general information only. Your policy, your agent and, if you disagree with a decision, an attorney or the NJ Department of Banking and Insurance are the right sources.
