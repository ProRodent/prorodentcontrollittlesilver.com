Scratching Noises in the Attic at Night: Mice or Rats?

A guide to the sounds, droppings and timing that separate mice from rats and other attic visitors in Little Silver Cape Cod homes.

Light, quick scratching and scurrying in the attic at night is usually mice. Heavier thumps, gnawing that sounds like dragging or rolling, and noise in the early evening point to rats or squirrels. Sound alone can mislead you, so confirm with droppings, timing and where the noise is coming from before you pick a fix.

What does each sound mean?

Mice weigh well under an ounce, so they make small, rapid patter, scratching, and occasional squeaks. You may hear it inside a wall or above a ceiling, often most active around dusk and again before dawn. It tends to move in short bursts and then stop.

A Norway rat is many times heavier and sounds like it. Expect louder thumping, gnawing on wood or pipes, and dragging or rolling sounds, sometimes at the same hours as mice. Rats are less common in attics than mice, since they prefer to travel low, but they climb well and use tree limbs and utility lines to reach a roof.

How do I check droppings and tracks?

Wear a mask and gloves, and look with a flashlight from the attic hatch instead of walking through the space. Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings, since that puts particles in the air. Photograph what you see, and measure a dropping against a ruler if you can do so safely. Size and shape tell you more than any noise.

Sprinkle a thin layer of flour or baby powder along a suspected route for a night. Tiny footprints with a tail drag suggest mice, larger prints suggest rats or squirrels, and no prints at all can mean the noise came from somewhere else. Dark greasy smears along beams and around gaps show a route used many times. When contamination is involved, attic and crawl space cleanup covers removal and sanitizing.

Droppings do not lie: measure one and you usually know which animal you are dealing with.

Why is the noise worse at night?

Mice and rats are mostly nocturnal, so they leave nests and hunt for food and water when the house is quiet. Household noise during the day covers a lot of what you would otherwise hear. Silence after bedtime makes even a light scratch obvious.

Timing also separates animals. Squirrels are active by day and go quiet at dusk, so scratching at 6 a.m. that stops by mid-morning fits a squirrel. Bats leave at dusk and come back near dawn. If the noise starts around midnight and runs in bursts until 4 a.m., think mice or rats.

Season helps as well. Mouse and rat noise builds in the cold months, when animals move toward warm framing, and it often follows the first hard cold snap of fall. Squirrel activity in spring and early summer may be a nesting female. If the sound started right after a storm or a stretch of very wet weather, look for new gaps at the roofline and vents.

What about Cape Cod attics and kneewalls?

Cape Cods are common in Little Silver, and many of them have a finished half-story upstairs with kneewalls. Those short side walls hide a long, narrow cavity behind them, usually with an access door, and rodents use these spaces as a highway. Noise that seems to be coming from the bedroom ceiling may actually be inside the kneewall space, so open the small access door and look with a flashlight.

Check the kneewall doors, the eave spaces and the gaps where the roofline meets the siding. Thin or settled insulation in these spaces is easy for mice to pull into nests. If you find droppings or shredded batting, do not enter the space without protection, and call for a rodent inspection to trace the entry point.

Use the room below to narrow it down. Stand there at night and note the spot where the sound is loudest, then mark it. Activity that stops and resumes in the same place is probably a nest or a regular route, and that spot tells a technician where to look first.

Could it be squirrels or bats?

Yes, and the difference matters. Squirrels are loud, mostly daytime animals that chew on roof edges, soffits and vents, and they are often mistaken for rats. Neither squirrels nor bats are a job for snap traps, so a professional should identify the animal before anything is closed or set.

Bats are protected in New Jersey, so do not seal, poison or trap them. Sealing bats inside during the maternity season, generally early summer, can strand pups and cause them to enter the living space. Call a professional or your state wildlife agency for guidance on timing and legal removal. Public guidance is general, so confirm local rules before acting.

What should I do tonight and tomorrow?

Attic noise action list.

  • Note the time the noise starts and stops, and whether it is louder at dusk, midnight or dawn.
  • Look from the hatch with a flashlight for droppings, nests, staining and light coming in from outside.
  • Measure and photograph droppings, and do not touch or sweep them.
  • Walk the outside at dusk and look for gaps, damaged vents and branches touching the roof.
  • If you suspect bats, do not seal anything, and call for professional advice.
  • If droppings are larger than a grain of rice or you hear heavy thumping, call about rat control.

For mice, the fix is trapping plus sealing, which our mouse control service covers from the attic to the foundation. Rats need a broader approach, since burrows outside the house are usually part of the problem. If you back onto a stream or the river, check the foundation for rat sign as well, because rats follow the water's edge.

Common questions

Can mice make loud noises in the attic?

Not usually. A single mouse is quiet, but a group running through insulation can sound surprisingly loud in a silent house. If the sounds are heavy or thumping, suspect rats or squirrels instead.

Do rats live in attics in New Jersey?

Sometimes. Norway rats prefer burrows and low spaces, but they can climb to a roof and enter through vents or gaps. Roof rats are uncommon in NJ. Droppings around half an inch long suggest a rat.

How do I know if it is a bat?

Bats make faint scratching or chirping near dusk and dawn and leave small crumbly droppings near an entry. Because they are protected in NJ, do not close their entry or use poison. Call a professional for guidance.

Can I call while the noise is happening?

Yes. Our number is answered around the clock, and we schedule same-day visits where possible. Describe what you hear and when it started, and we can advise you on the next step.

Is it safe to sleep with rodents in the attic?

Rodents contaminate insulation and can chew wiring, which can contribute to fire risk, so get the noise checked soon. Talk to a physician about any health worries.
