When Do Mice Come Inside in New Jersey? Fall Timeline

Mice start moving into Little Silver homes when September nights cool off, and this month-by-month plan shows what to do before they arrive.

In New Jersey, mice begin moving indoors from about mid-September and the push peaks through October and early November, once nights turn cool and outdoor food thins out. The exact week depends on the weather, not the calendar. A run of cold, wet nights sends them inside within days, so finish exterior sealing by the end of September.

What triggers mice to move inside?

Mice do not migrate. They live in a small territory, often only a modest patch of yard, and stay there while food and shelter hold up. When fall arrives, three things change at once: nights turn cool, seed and insects disappear, and the leaf litter and garden beds that sheltered them get thin or wet.

Your house then looks like the best deal in the neighborhood. It is warm and dry, it has food in pantries, pet bowls and garages, and it has many gaps at the foundation and roofline. Any opening about 1/4 inch wide is enough. Once inside, mice breed year-round, so a few early arrivals in October can become a large group by midwinter.

What should I do in September?

September is the easiest month to act because the mice are not inside yet. Walk the outside of the house with a flashlight and look at every place something passes through the wall: gas and water lines, cable entries, dryer and bath vents, and the gap under the garage door. Our guide on how small a hole a mouse can fit through helps you judge what counts.

Seal what you find with hardware cloth or copper mesh, then mortar or a sealant rated for the surface. Replace worn door sweeps and screen crawl-space and attic vents. A rodent exclusion visit makes sense for anything on the roofline, where a ladder and roof access matter more than skill.

What should I do in October?

October is when the first mice actually arrive, usually after the first cold snap. Put snap traps against the walls in the garage, basement corners and behind the kitchen appliances now, baited with a small dab of peanut butter. Traps in place before the first sighting catch the scouts before they call others in.

Bring firewood, pumpkins, corn stalks and bagged leaf litter away from the house, and store them off the ground. Move pet food and bird seed indoors into sealed metal or heavy plastic bins. If you find droppings, use the CDC-based cleanup steps rather than sweeping.

Harvest season adds its own draw. Fallen fruit, bird feeders, unpicked tomatoes and stored squash all attract mice and give them a reason to stay near the house. Pick up windfalls every few days, and hang feeders well away from the walls, since the seed that drops below them feeds mice all night.

The best trap is a sealed foundation. The second best is one set before the first mouse.

How do gardens and leaf drop change the risk in Little Silver?

Little Silver was farmland and nursery land before it was a commuter suburb, and the borough still has a lot of gardens, mature shrubs and planted beds. Those give mice cover right up against foundations. Dense ivy, mulch piled against siding and hedges that touch the house all hide the runways mice use to reach a gap. Cut plants back so there is at least a foot of open ground next to the foundation.

Autumn cleanup can help or hurt. Cutting back perennials and raking leaves removes cover, but a pile of leaves or a compost heap next to the house creates a new shelter. Move both to the far side of the yard, and if the garden feeds rodents all season, our backyard and outdoor rodent control service covers the areas outside the walls.

How do coastal weather swings affect the timing?

Coastal New Jersey can swing from a warm afternoon to a cold, wet night in the same day, and a nor'easter can arrive in October. Rain and wind push mice out of burrows and leaf litter and toward dry framing. Low, damp areas near the Shrewsbury River and its streams flood at high tide, so a wet week can move mice sooner than the calendar suggests.

That means you should not wait for a cold date. Use the forecast: when several cool nights in a row are coming or a storm is on the way, that week is your deadline for sealing and trapping. After a flood, follow the advice in our post on rodents after storm flooding.

Sills, siding joints and utility openings that have weathered many seasons can open small gaps. Check those spots after any big weather event, since wet-dry cycles and a hard freeze can reopen a gap you closed in September.

What should I do in November?

By November, mice that have not been stopped are already nesting. Listen at dusk and before dawn for scratching in walls or ceilings, and check the attic and garage for droppings, chewed insulation and shredded material. Kitchens and pantries are the next targets, so go through the food supplies for gnawed corners.

If traps keep catching mice, or you notice new signs a week after sealing, there is likely an entry point you missed or a nest inside a wall. A mouse control visit can find both. For a garage or basement that is taking the brunt, garage and basement rodent control is a targeted option.

Fall prep checklist.

  • Late September: Walk the outside with a flashlight and mark every gap wider than a pencil.
  • Late September: Seal gaps with mesh and mortar, screen vents, and repair door sweeps.
  • Early October: Set snap traps along garage and basement walls before you see any droppings.
  • Early October: Move firewood, compost, leaf piles and bird feeders away from the house.
  • Late October: Check traps daily and store all pet food in sealed bins.
  • November: Inspect attic and garage for droppings, and call if signs continue after sealing.

Common questions

What is the first sign mice have moved in for fall?

Small dark droppings along baseboards, in drawers or behind appliances are the most common first sign. You may also hear light scratching at night or find gnawed food packaging. Any of these in September or October means the sealing window has probably closed.

Is it too late to seal my house in November?

No, but do it in the right order. Set traps first so mice are not sealed inside, then close gaps. Sealing after mice are inside can trap them in walls, so check with a technician if you are unsure.

Do mice come in only when it gets cold?

They can enter any time there is a gap, and warm, dry weather is not a reason to relax. Cold nights and rain simply give them a reason to look. Some homes get mice all year, especially near gardens and woods.

Can you help if I hear mice at 2 a.m.?

Yes. Our line is open around the clock, and where the schedule allows we visit the same day. Call any time of night, and we can talk through what you are hearing and set up a visit.

Will a mild autumn mean fewer mice indoors?

Usually the move comes later, not less. A mild October delays entry, and then the first hard cold or storm sends a rush inside in a few days. Prepare on the same schedule regardless.
