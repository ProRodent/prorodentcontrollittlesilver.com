A rodent sighting is a business problem, not only a pest problem.

One mouse in a dining room or stockroom can cost you a shift, a shipment and a customer's trust. Rodents contaminate food, chew packaging and leave droppings that a health inspector will notice before you do. A program that catches activity early is usually easier to manage than a crisis.

We build commercial service around three things: inspection that finds why rodents are there, monitoring that shows activity before it spreads, and records you can hand to an inspector. For the first look, rodent inspection works the same way for a shop as for a house.

Prospect Avenue and Church Street: where the risks sit.

Prospect Avenue is the borough's Main Street, with restaurants, specialty shops and older buildings. Church Street has small shops in restored older buildings. Any building that mixes food, packaging, back doors and dumpsters in a tight space gives rodents what they look for, so on these streets we start at the back door and the trash area.

Older buildings often share walls, and a gap in a neighbor's wall can become your problem. Rodents travel along shared framing, pipe chases and utility lines. So we check between units as well as inside yours, and we tell you what we find on the shared side. Ask your landlord who is responsible for shared walls before a problem starts.

Food handling and the local health inspector.

Food businesses are inspected, and inspectors look for evidence of pests: droppings, gnaw marks, nesting and entry points. We will not quote regulations we cannot verify, so ask the local health department what applies to your license. What we can say is that a clean, documented program helps you answer their questions.

Keep records of every service visit, what was found, what was done and what is planned. Businesses should keep that documentation on file, and we provide a written summary after each visit so you have it ready.

If it is not written down, an inspector may assume it was not done.

Monitoring visits and what we document.

A monitoring visit is not a quick spray. A technician checks stations, inspects the interior and exterior, looks for fresh signs and updates a map of activity. We then note conditions that invite rodents and recommend fixes. Between visits, staff can jot new signs in a simple log so nothing waits for the next check.

  • Station checks: we inspect each monitoring station and record any activity.
  • Interior and exterior walk-through: we look at doors, drains, storage and the dumpster area.
  • Findings and actions: we note what we saw and what we did, in writing.
  • Recommendations: we list sanitation, storage and structural fixes you can act on.

When we find a gap, we discuss closing it through rodent exclusion, because trapping alone never solves a building that lets them in.

Staff habits that keep rodents out.

Most commercial infestations grow through small daily habits. Doors propped open during deliveries, boxes left on the floor overnight, and food scraps left near the back door all draw rodents. A short conversation with your staff often does more than any device.

Habits worth building into your routine.

  • Close the back door between trips, and check the sweep for daylight along the bottom.
  • Store food and dry goods off the floor and away from walls, in sealed containers.
  • Break down boxes and take them out daily, and keep dumpster lids closed.
  • Report droppings, gnawing or sightings to a manager the same day, and note the time and place.

Deliveries, dumpsters and the back of the building.

Behind a restaurant or shop, the alley or rear lot is often where rodents get their start. Rats burrow near dumpsters and along foundations, and mice ride in on cardboard. Walk the back of your building once a week and look for burrow holes, greasy rub marks along walls and spilled food.

If rats are the concern, rat control explains how we track burrows and place tamper-resistant stations. Ask your waste hauler about lid condition and pickup timing too, since an overflowing bin between pickups feeds rats all night.

After-hours response.

Rodent activity does not keep business hours. If you open in the morning and find droppings on the prep table, you need an answer before the doors open. Our 24/7 line takes commercial calls, and we try to book a same-day visit around your day.

Questions about commercial rodent control

Can you work around our opening hours?

Yes. We can plan visits before you open or after you close so service does not disrupt customers, and we can respond outside those hours when activity is urgent.

What do you give us for the health inspector?

A written summary after each visit covering what we found, what we did and what we recommend. Inspectors look for evidence of pests, so businesses should keep these records on file.

What if the problem is coming from the shop next door?

Shared walls make that common. We inspect the shared side where access allows, tell you what we find, and suggest you speak with your landlord or neighbor about sealing on their side.

Do you use bait in a kitchen?

We prefer monitoring and exclusion in food areas. Any bait we use goes in tamper-resistant stations, placed out of reach of children, pets and wildlife, and follows the product label.

Can a delivery bring rodents in?

Yes. Rodents can ride in on boxes and pallets, or slip in an open door. Check deliveries before you store them, and keep the door closed between trips.

What should I do if I see a rodent during service hours?

Stay calm, note where it went, and keep customers away from the area. Call us and describe the time and place, which helps us decide where to look first.
