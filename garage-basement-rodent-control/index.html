Why lower levels get hit first in a low-lying borough.

Little Silver sits at roughly 16 feet of elevation, and streets like Seven Bridges Road and Prospect Avenue are known for tidal flooding at high tide. Water pushes rodents out of burrows and drainage lines, and the nearest dry, sheltered space is often a garage or a basement. A lower level that sits partly below grade puts the wall against soil, right where mice and rats travel.

That is why we start at the bottom of the house, not the attic. Most rodents in a lower level came in at ground height: a warped garage door edge, a gap where a pipe passes through the wall, or the seam under the sill plate. If you hear scratching overhead instead, attic and crawl space cleanup is likely the better first stop.

Where they get in: a quick guide to lower-level entry points.

A house mouse fits through a gap of about 1/4 inch, and a rat needs about 1/2 inch. Those are smaller than most people expect, and a garage or basement has plenty of them. This table shows what we check first and how each spot is normally closed.

Cardboard, bird seed and pet food: what we ask you to move.

Stored cardboard is one of the best nesting materials a rodent can find. It is dry, easy to shred, and stacked in a dark corner nobody visits for months. Mice will tunnel through a pile of boxes and line the middle with paper, so we often find a nest only after a box is lifted. If a stack has not moved since spring, treat it as suspect and lift it with gloves on. A finished basement adds drop ceilings and wall cavities, and we check those edges too.

Bird seed and dog or cat food kept in bags in the garage are the food source that keeps them there. A rodent can chew through a paper or thin plastic bag in one night. Move seed and pet food into metal or thick lidded plastic bins, and switch from cardboard to sealed plastic totes for anything you plan to keep.

Trapping without sealing just resets the clock. Sealing without trapping locks the survivors in with your stuff.

What a garage and basement visit involves.

A technician inspects the exterior first, then the garage, then the lower level, looking for droppings, rub marks along walls, gnaw damage and burrow openings beside the slab. We identify whether you have mice, rats or both, because the traps, bait station placement and gap sizes are different for each.

Trapping and any bait stations go in along the runways we found, not scattered around the room. Bait, where it is used at all, goes in tamper-resistant stations placed away from children, pets and wildlife. We do not leave loose poison in a garage, since a pet or a child can reach it. Once activity drops, rodent exclusion work closes the entry points so new animals cannot follow.

Set up the lower level first.

  • Pull stored boxes a foot or two away from the walls so we can see the baseboard line and the sill plate.
  • Note where you saw droppings, chewed packaging or greasy rub marks, and roughly how fresh they looked.
  • Move pet food and bird seed to a sealed container and put out only what animals finish in one sitting.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings. Leave them for us or follow the wet-cleanup steps from the CDC.
  • Keep pets and children out of the area until the visit is done.

After a storm surge or high-water week.

Hurricane Sandy in October 2012 flooded low bayside streets across Monmouth County, and the borough's low waterfront streets still see tidal flooding. After any event that fills a yard or drainage line, displaced rats can test basement walls, sump pits and garage thresholds within days.

If you are in a low block, check the sump lid, the dryer vent flap and any old pipe holes after high water recedes. Wet insulation and stored boxes also hold odor if an animal has died behind them. Our dead rodent removal and odor control service covers that situation, and our rat control page explains how we handle burrows along foundations.

Questions about garage and basement rodent control

Can mice really get in through a closed garage door?

Yes. A gap of about 1/4 inch is enough, and a worn bottom seal or bent corner easily leaves that much. If you can see daylight under the door or along the sides, a mouse can use it. We replace the seals and check the threshold where the slab is uneven.

What if I hear something in the garage at 2 a.m.?

Call the 24/7 line. We will tell you what to do until a technician can get there, and we offer a same-day visit when the day has room.

My basement smells musty and I found droppings. Is that a rodent problem or a moisture problem?

It can be both. Damp lower levels attract rodents, and rodents add their own odor. Droppings about 1/8 to 1/4 inch and pointed suggest mice, while blunt droppings near 1/2 inch or more suggest rats. Tell us what you see and we will sort it out on site.

Is it safe to store bird seed in the garage?

Only in a metal can or a heavy lidded bin. Loose bags of seed are a steady food source, and spilled seed under a feeder draws rodents right to your door. If you had activity last season, keep the seed elsewhere until we finish.

Will the traps be dangerous for my dog or cat?

We place traps and any bait stations in protected spots and follow the product label. Bait goes in tamper-resistant stations, and we tell you where each device sits. Tell us about your pets on the call so we can plan around them.

Why do you check the dryer vent and sump pit?

Both are openings that lead straight outside. A dryer vent with a broken flap or missing screen is an easy entrance, and an uncovered sump pit gives rats a drain path into the basement. Closing them is a routine part of the job.

Do I need to throw out everything that was stored near the droppings?

Not always. Cardboard and paper that were nested in or soiled should go. Sealed plastic items can usually be wiped down with disinfectant after you wear gloves and ventilate the space. If you are unsure about something, ask us or your physician for health questions.
