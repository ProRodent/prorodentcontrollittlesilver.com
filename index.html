How rodent control works in Little Silver

Most calls we take here fall into a few patterns. Scratching in an attic or wall at night. Droppings in a kitchen drawer or garage. A dead-animal smell that will not fade. Chewed wires in a car. Burrow holes along a foundation or under a shed. Each one points to a different fix.

Mice and rats need different handling. Mice are small, breed all year indoors and slip through gaps near 1/4 inch, so they favor attics, wall voids and pantries. Rats are larger, burrow, and follow water and food waste. Our mouse control and rat control visits start from that split.

Little Silver makes the split matter. About 8% of homes sit directly on the Shrewsbury River and roughly a third sit on streams that connect to it. Rats travel those banks and test foundations first. Homes farther inland, many of them mid-century builds, tend to see more mice in attics and eaves.

The service follows one order. We inspect to find the entry points and the species, treat the active animals, seal the gaps, and follow up to confirm the activity has stopped. A rodent inspection can be the first step when you do not know where they get in.

Trapping alone rarely ends a problem, because new animals move in through the same gaps. That is why rodent exclusion is part of the plan. If the noise is a night-time emergency, calling the line directly is the fastest route.

Our four-step approach

  1. 1

    Inspect

    We walk the exterior and interior, checking rooflines, vents, utility lines and foundation edges. On river-side homes we look first at the side facing the water, where rats often test the foundation.

  2. 2

    Treat

    We remove active animals with traps and tamper-resistant stations placed out of reach of children and pets. We avoid loose bait, and we explain what is placed and where before we leave.

  3. 3

    Seal

    We close entry points with materials rodents cannot chew through, such as hardware cloth, sheet metal and mortar. Steel wool alone does not last. Chimney caps and vent screens are common fixes on older homes.

  4. 4

    Follow up

    We return to check traps and stations and confirm the noise, droppings and gnawing have stopped. If activity continues, we look for the gap we missed instead of simply adding more traps.

Signs you have mice or rats

Rodents leave clues long before you see one. These six are the ones we hear about most.

  • Droppings

    Mouse droppings are about 1/8 to 1/4 inch and pointed. Rat droppings run roughly 1/2 to 3/4 inch and blunt. Do not sweep them dry.

  • Gnaw marks

    Fresh chew marks on wood, plastic, wiring or food packaging look pale and rough. Chewed wiring can contribute to fire risk.

  • Night noises

    Scratching, scurrying or squeaking in walls and ceilings after dark usually means active nesting close by.

  • Nests

    Shredded paper, fabric and insulation packed into corners, boxes or engine bays point to a nest and a breeding site.

  • Grease marks

    Dark, oily smears along baseboards and pipes show a runway that rodents use again and again.

  • Odors

    A musty ammonia smell suggests heavy activity. A sharp rotting smell in a wall often means a dead animal.

Seen even one of these signs? Call now and we will tell you what to do first.

Why rodents find Little Silver homes

Parker Homestead, early 1700s

The Parker Homestead is on the National Register of Historic Places and is one of the oldest dwellings standing in New Jersey. Buildings of that age, and older homes generally, often have masonry foundations and settled framing with many small gaps. If your house is old, plan on a full exterior sealing pass.

Little Silver train station

The 1890 station sits on the commuter line, and the streets around it mix older homes with townhouses. Tight lots and shared walls let rodents move between properties. Tell us if a neighbor has had activity so we check shared edges.

Santelle Park and Boat Launch

The public boat launch on Riverview Avenue is a working part of the Shrewsbury River shoreline. Waterfront edges are where rats travel, so if you live near the shore, keep trash lidded and remove pet food overnight.

The borough grew from farms, marshes and Lovett's Nursery, once described as the East Coast's largest. That left fertile soil and a long tradition of gardens and heavy landscaping. Dense shrubs, mulch and bird feeders give rodents cover and food, so we trim plantings back from foundations and move feeders away from the house.

Rail service helped turn the area into a commuter suburb, and the housing that followed is now mostly mid-century Capes, split-levels and colonials, with newer rebuilds mixed in. Older framing has more gaps at sills, eaves and utility lines. A mouse needs only about 1/4 inch, so an exterior check of these points comes first.

Water shapes the rest. The borough sits near 16 feet of elevation, and Seven Bridges Road and Prospect Avenue are known for tidal flooding at high tide. Flooding pushes rats out of burrows and toward higher ground, sometimes into garages and crawl spaces. After a bad tide, check low areas for fresh droppings.

Rodent control questions from Little Silver homeowners

Do you respond at night or on weekends?

Yes. Someone answers around the clock, and same-day visits are offered when bookings permit. If animals are active in your living space at night, call right away.

How do I tell mice from rats?

Check droppings and gaps. Mouse droppings are small and pointed, and mice fit through 1/4 inch openings. Rat droppings are larger and blunt, and rats often burrow outside near foundations, sheds and water.

Why do traps alone fail?

Traps remove the animals you have today but do not close the gaps. New rodents follow scent trails in through the same openings. Sealing entry points is what keeps the problem from returning.

Is treatment safe for pets and children?

We use tamper-resistant bait stations and place them out of reach of children, pets and wildlife. Tell us about pets and where they roam, and we will choose placements around them.

What should I have ready when I call?

Your street, where you hear or see activity, when it started, and whether you have seen droppings, chewing or dead animals. Mention pets, children and any recent flooding.

Do you handle commercial properties?

Yes. Restaurants, shops and offices along Prospect Avenue and Church Street face the same pressures as homes. Our commercial rodent control service covers them. Call and describe the building.

How can I check a pest company's license?

In New Jersey, commercial pesticide applicators must be licensed through the NJDEP Pesticide Control Program. Ask any provider for their license number and confirm it with NJDEP before work begins.
