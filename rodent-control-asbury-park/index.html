What we handle on a typical call.

General mice and rat activity leads the list, as it does everywhere we cover. Right behind it are droppings cleanup requests, often from someone who found evidence in a kitchen or storage area and wants it handled safely rather than swept up loose.

We also get calls about rodent damage, where someone wants an estimate for repairing chewed wiring or insulation after an infestation is cleared. That estimate work ties into our rodent damage repair page. Attic and wall activity, including scratching heard at night, rounds out most of the remaining calls.

How the visit works.

Every visit starts with an inspection, whether the property is a house, a rental unit or a storefront. A technician looks for droppings, gnaw marks and entry points before any trap goes down, which is the same process described on our rodent inspection and estimate page.

From there we place traps or stations along the routes we actually find rodents using, then seal the gaps that let them in. For commercial sites, we work with the schedule you give us, since foot traffic and food-safety rules can affect timing. A follow-up visit confirms the traps are clear before we close the job.

Odor and vehicle calls.

A lingering smell with no visible source is a common call in older buildings and multi-unit housing, and we search for the source rather than masking it, covered on our dead rodent removal and odor control page. Rodents in a parked vehicle come up too, especially near garages and lots, and that work is described on our vehicle and car rodent removal page.

Working around a business schedule.

A storefront cannot always close for an inspection during peak hours, so we ask about your schedule up front and plan the visit around it where possible. Food-service locations in particular need entry points sealed carefully and treatment kept away from prep and storage areas, and we build the plan around those constraints rather than treating a store like an ordinary house.

Recurring plans for repeat activity.

Some property owners, especially those managing a store or a multi-unit building, ask about a recurring visit schedule rather than a single call. We can talk through that option once the initial problem is under control, based on how the property has behaved historically.

Emergency situations in a busy city.

A city with dense housing and active storefronts sees its share of urgent calls, whether that is a rodent seen during business hours, wiring damage discovered right before an inspection, or a tenant reporting activity the night before a move-in. Our line stays open for those calls, and we judge urgency based on what you tell us rather than a fixed schedule.

Before we arrive.

  • Keep pets, children and staff away from any area with droppings or signs of activity.
  • Note where and when you saw or heard rodents, including which unit or area if it is a multi-unit building.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings before the visit.
  • Do not set out loose bait or poison on your own.
  • Clear access to the stockroom, attic, garage or crawl space where activity is concentrated.

Sources and standards
