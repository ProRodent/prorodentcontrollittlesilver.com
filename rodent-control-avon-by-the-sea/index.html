What we handle on calls from this stretch of shore.

Mice and rats generally are the most common reason for a call, followed by droppings that show up in a kitchen or closet and need to be cleaned up safely. Dead-animal odor is another frequent reason, especially in homes with attic space above living areas.

We also get calls asking for a pet-safe approach when dogs or cats share the home, and requests for a plan that covers the property on a recurring basis rather than a single visit. Our rodent trapping and removal service covers the active side, and rodent exclusion handles the sealing that keeps activity from returning.

How the visit is structured.

We inspect first, checking the roofline, vents, foundation and any gaps around pipes or wiring. A mouse only needs about a quarter inch of opening, so we look closely rather than just at the obvious spots.

From there we treat what's active, seal what's open, and return to confirm the work held. If odor is the concern, that step includes locating and removing the source, not just masking the smell in the room.

Sealing an older or waterfront home.

Older homes near the water can have more small gaps at sills and utility lines than newer construction. Steel wool alone does not hold up to chewing, so we use hardware cloth, sheet metal and mortar, along with door sweeps and vent screens where those are the weak points.

A note on timing and the season.

Cooling nights from September through November send mice looking for a warmer place to nest, and that shift touches shore towns as much as inland ones. Homes closed up for the off-season can go weeks without anyone noticing early activity.

If you own a seasonal property here, a check before you close it up for the year and again before you reopen it can catch a small problem before it grows. Tell us the schedule when you call, and we will plan around it.

Standard homeowners insurance often excludes rodent damage, so it is worth checking your policy or asking your agent, especially if chewed wiring or insulation is part of what brought you to call.

Norway rats are the common outdoor species along this part of the coast, and they burrow near foundations, decks and storm drains where water and food waste collect. Checking those low spots after heavy rain or a high tide is a reasonable habit here.

Mice, by contrast, breed indoors all year and favor attics, wall voids and pantries over burrows in the yard. Knowing which pattern fits what you are seeing helps us plan the first step before we even arrive.

Before we arrive.

  • Keep pets and children away from the area with activity.
  • Note where and when you saw or heard rodents.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings.
  • Do not set out loose bait on your own before we arrive.
  • Clear access to the attic or crawl space if you're able to.

How long does a dead rodent smell usually last?

It often fades over roughly one to three weeks depending on size and location once the animal is removed and the area is treated, though it can vary.

Can flooding push rodents into a home?

Yes, high water can push rats and mice out of low areas and toward higher ground, sometimes into garages or crawl spaces. Checking low spots after a bad tide is a good habit.
