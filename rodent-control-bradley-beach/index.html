How a visit works here.

We inspect the property first, checking rooflines, vents, crawl space openings and foundation edges for gaps. Openings near a quarter inch wide are enough for a mouse, so we look closely at small ones, not just obvious holes.

From there we treat what is active using traps and tamper-resistant stations placed along real travel routes, then seal the entry points with hardware cloth, sheet metal or mortar depending on the gap. A final follow-up confirms the activity has actually stopped, not just paused.

If the property is a small shop or rental unit rather than a single house, our commercial rodent control service follows the same order with timing built around business hours.

Rodent biology, in plain terms.

Mice breed year-round indoors, and a female can have five to ten litters a year with around five or six pups each. A pair that moves in during a quiet month can turn into a much bigger problem within a season.

Norway rats are the common outdoor species in this part of New Jersey. They burrow under decks, sheds and slabs, and they follow water and food waste, which is part of why yards near storm drains or low spots draw more activity.

A mouse can squeeze through a gap about a quarter inch wide, and a rat through roughly half an inch. Both numbers are smaller than most people expect, which is why an exclusion check often finds openings a homeowner had never noticed.

Checking a provider's license.

In New Jersey, commercial pesticide applicators must be licensed through the NJDEP Pesticide Control Program. It is reasonable to ask any provider for their license number and confirm it with the state before work starts on your property.

Before we arrive.

  • Keep pets and children away from the area with activity.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings.
  • Do not put out loose bait on your own before we arrive.
  • Note where and when you saw or heard rodents.
  • Clear access to the attic, crawl space or garage if you're able to.
