Coverage for Eatontown borough.

Eatontown is an incorporated borough in Monmouth County. We are based in Little Silver, not in Eatontown itself, and calls from Eatontown addresses are handled the same way as any town on our regular coverage list.

Because the distance is short, same-day visits are possible on most days. Tell us your street when you call so we can plan the route and give a realistic arrival window rather than a vague one.

We do not treat a business call as less urgent than a home call, or the other way around. Both get the same inspection-first approach, just scheduled around whatever hours make sense for the property.

Homes and businesses we take calls from.

A mix of property types call our line from this area, from single-family homes to offices and stores. Homeowners tend to call about scratching in an attic, droppings in a kitchen or pantry, or a bad smell they cannot locate. Businesses call more often about activity in a stockroom or storage area, or want a commercial rodent control visit scheduled around store hours.

We also hear from people whose car picked up a rodent over the winter, with chewed wiring or nesting material in the engine bay, and from residents preparing to move who want the house checked and cleared first.

Some calls involve a combination of pests, like mice and ants showing up around the same time near a kitchen. We handle both where it makes sense, instead of splitting the visit into two separate appointments.

How the visit is structured.

Every visit follows the same order. We inspect to find entry points and confirm what is active, treat with traps and tamper-resistant stations, seal the gaps we found, and return to confirm the activity has stopped. Skipping the sealing step is the most common reason a rodent problem comes back.

For an inspection-only visit, such as before a sale or a lease renewal, our rodent inspection and estimate service gives you a written plan without committing to treatment on the spot.

Attic and wall activity in older homes.

Homes built decades ago tend to have more small gaps around plumbing chases, wiring runs and where the roof meets the walls. A mouse only needs a gap of about 1/4 inch to get through, and a rat can fit through something closer to the size of a quarter, so these small openings matter more than people expect.

If your attic has had insulation disturbed, or you notice shredded paper or fabric packed into a corner, that usually points to a nest rather than just animals passing through. Mention that on the call so we know to focus there first.

If you are unsure whether a rodent issue is minor or something bigger, describe it anyway. We would rather rule out a small problem on a quick call than have activity go unchecked for weeks.

What helps us on the call.

Tell us whether the property is a home or a business, roughly where the activity is, and if pets, children or employees use that space regularly. That is usually enough for us to schedule the right kind of visit.

If you are an agent calling on behalf of a client, such as a buyer, seller or landlord, tell us that role too. We can direct findings to whoever needs them for the transaction.

Before we arrive.

  • Keep pets, children or employees away from areas with signs of activity.
  • Note where and when you noticed droppings, noise or smell.
  • Do not sweep dry droppings before the visit.
  • Do not put out loose bait or poison on your own.
  • Clear access to storage areas, attic or garage if you can do so safely.
