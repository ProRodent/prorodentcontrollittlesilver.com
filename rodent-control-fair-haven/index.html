How a visit works here.

We follow the same process for every property we visit: inspect first, treat what is active, seal the gaps, then follow up. Inspection means checking the roofline, foundation, vents and any spot with droppings or noise, so the plan matches what is actually happening rather than guesswork.

Treatment relies on traps and tamper-resistant stations placed on the routes rodents actually use, not loose bait scattered around. Once the active animals are handled, we close the openings we found, using materials rodents cannot chew through, and a later visit confirms the activity has stopped for good.

We treat every property the same way regardless of size, whether it is a small starter home or a larger house with a finished basement. The inspection scope changes with the building, but the order of steps does not.

Calls we take from this area.

A lot of calls start the same way: scratching overhead at night, or droppings turning up in a kitchen or garage. Others call about a smell that will not go away, which often means an animal died somewhere in a wall or duct. We also hear from people whose vehicle picked up a rodent nest over a cold snap, engine wiring chewed through, or nesting material packed into the engine bay.

Some calls are less about an emergency and more about prevention, like a homeowner who wants entry points sealed before winter, or someone getting a house ready to list who wants an inspection and estimate first. Whatever the reason, tell us plainly on the call and we will match it to the right visit.

We also get calls about mixed activity, where mice and ants show up in the same season, usually near a kitchen or a damaged screen. We can address both in the same visit or point you toward the right combination of services depending on what we find.

Common questions on the phone.

People often ask whether they should try snap traps from the hardware store first. That can work for a single mouse, but if the activity keeps up after a week or two, it usually means the entry point is still open and new rodents keep arriving. At that stage a full inspection is a better use of time than more traps.

Others ask about recurring service, especially after dealing with the same problem more than once. A yearly or seasonal check can catch new gaps before they turn into an active problem, and we can talk through what that would look like for your property.

What to have ready when you call.

Have a rough idea of where you noticed activity, whether pets or children use that area, and how long it has been going on. None of that needs to be exact. It just helps us plan the first visit instead of arriving with no direction.

If someone else will be home for the visit, such as a family member or a contractor, let us know who to expect and how to reach them if plans change.

Before we arrive.

  • Keep pets and children away from the area where you noticed activity.
  • Note where and when you saw or heard rodents.
  • Do not sweep dry droppings. Wait for guidance on safe cleanup.
  • Do not put out loose bait or poison before we arrive.
  • Clear access to the attic, crawl space or garage if you can do so safely.

What if I hear rodents but never see one?

That is common. Scratching at night, droppings, or gnaw marks are usually enough for us to start an inspection without you having spotted the animal itself.

Can rodents get into a car parked outside?

Yes, especially in cooler months. Rodents chew wiring and insulation and build nests in engine bays. Our vehicle rodent removal service handles that separately from house calls.

Do you offer a pre-listing inspection?

Yes. If you or your agent wants a rodent inspection before a home goes on the market, our inspection and estimate service can be scheduled ahead of a listing date.

Is treatment safe around pets and kids?

We avoid loose bait and use tamper-resistant stations and traps placed out of reach. Tell us about any pets or children in the home so we plan placement accordingly.
