How a Farmingdale visit is run.

We start with an inspection, looking at rooflines, vents, foundation edges and gaps around pipes and wiring. Openings near a quarter inch are enough for a mouse to use, so we check small gaps as closely as obvious ones.

From there we treat active rodents with traps and tamper-resistant stations set along real travel routes, then close the entry points with hardware cloth, sheet metal or mortar depending on the gap. A follow-up visit confirms the activity has actually stopped.

If chewed wiring or wood is part of the concern, our rodent damage repair service can give you an estimate for that part of the work separately.

Cleaning up droppings the right way.

If you find droppings before we arrive, resist the urge to sweep or vacuum them, since that can stir up dust that carries health risks. Ventilate the room for about 30 minutes first, then wear gloves and wet the droppings with a disinfectant or a mild bleach solution before wiping them up.

Bag the paper towels and dispose of them, then wash your hands well. The CDC links rodent droppings to illnesses such as salmonellosis, so it is worth following that process rather than a quick sweep with a broom.

We can walk through the same steps with you on the phone if you would rather confirm before you start cleaning.

A mouse can fit through a gap about a quarter inch wide, roughly the width of a pencil, and a rat through about half an inch. Both are easy to miss during a quick look, which is why we check closely around utility lines, vents and foundation edges during an inspection.

Rodents are linked to health concerns such as salmonellosis and contamination of stored food, according to the CDC, so a slow response can turn a small nuisance into a bigger household concern.

If you are unsure whether what you are hearing is mice, rats or something else entirely, describe it to us on the call. We would rather ask a few extra questions upfront than plan the wrong visit.

Before we arrive.

  • Keep pets and children away from the area with activity.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings.
  • Do not set out loose bait before the visit.
  • Note where and when you saw or heard rodents.
  • Clear access to the attic hatch or garage storage if you can.

How do I tell mice from rats around the house?

Mouse droppings are small and pointed, about an eighth to a quarter inch. Rat droppings are larger and blunt, and rats tend to burrow outside near foundations or sheds.

Can traps alone solve the problem?

Usually not for long. Traps remove what's there, but new rodents can use the same gap unless it's sealed. We pair trapping with exclusion for that reason.

Do you handle a mixed mice-and-ant call?

Often, yes. Some properties have both at once since they can share the same entry point. We inspect for both rather than assuming it's only one issue.

Is treatment safe for kids and pets?
