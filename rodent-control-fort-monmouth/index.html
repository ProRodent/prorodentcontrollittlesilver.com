Fort Monmouth is not an ordinary town.

Fort Monmouth was a US Army installation for most of the 20th century and closed as an active base years ago. The site now spans parts of Eatontown, Oceanport and Tinton Falls, and it is mid-redevelopment into housing, retail and office space rather than being a single incorporated town with its own borough hall.

That matters for what a page like this can honestly say. We are not going to describe Fort Monmouth as a typical residential suburb, because it is not one yet. It is a former military property in transition, and the buildings, tenants and vacant parcels on it change as redevelopment moves forward.

Coverage for the Fort Monmouth footprint.

Our coverage list includes the ZIP 07703 area associated with the former post, which sits close to our Little Silver base. We take calls for rodent problems from residential units, offices, retail spaces and other buildings inside that footprint, the same way we cover any nearby town.

Because the site mixes new construction, renovated Army-era buildings and land still being developed, the rodent issues we see vary more than in an older settled neighborhood. Vacant or partially finished buildings can attract rodents looking for shelter, and active construction sites can push them toward nearby occupied units.

A site like this can also see turnover as leases end and new tenants move in, and an empty unit between tenants is a window when rodents can move in unnoticed. If you manage turnover for a unit here, a quick check between tenants can catch a problem before someone new signs a lease.

What we handle here.

Most calls from redevelopment-adjacent properties involve either mice or rats moving between vacant space and occupied buildings, or rodents that got into a vehicle parked near construction or storage areas. Droppings in a stockroom, scratching above a ceiling tile, or an odor from a wall void are all reasons people call us about a property in transition.

For an office, retail unit or mixed-use building, our commercial rodent control service covers inspection and treatment without disrupting a workday more than necessary. For a residential unit, the approach follows the same inspect, treat, seal, follow up model we use everywhere.

How a visit works.

We start by walking the property, inside and out, to find where rodents are entering and what is currently active. We treat with traps and tamper-resistant stations rather than loose bait, especially in a mixed-use setting where pets, children or coworkers may be nearby.

Once activity is under control, we seal the entry points we found, since a redevelopment site can have more temporary gaps around construction seams and utility work than a finished building. A follow-up check confirms the fix held before we close out the job.

What to tell us on the call.

Say whether your property is residential, commercial or on a vehicle, and mention if it sits near active construction or a vacant building, since that can be a source of activity. The clearer the picture, the better we can plan the first visit.

If you manage a property on behalf of a redevelopment authority, a landlord or a tenant, tell us that role up front. We are used to coordinating with a property manager rather than the person who actually lives or works in the unit, and it helps to know who signs off on the visit.

Keeping the page honest.

We are not going to list specific buildings, streets or tenants on this page, because the site changes as redevelopment continues and we do not want to publish anything that goes stale or wrong. If you are unsure whether your address falls inside the covered footprint, the fastest way to check is to call and give us the address directly.

Before we arrive.

  • Keep pets, children and coworkers away from any area with signs of activity.
  • Note where and when you saw or heard rodents, and near which building or unit.
  • Do not sweep dry droppings. Leave the area until we can advise on safe cleanup.
  • Do not set out loose bait on your own, especially in a shared or commercial space.
  • Clear access to storage rooms, crawl spaces or the area near a parked vehicle if you can.
