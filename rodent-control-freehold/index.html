What we handle on Freehold calls.

Calls from Freehold Borough cover the usual range: general mice or rats, droppings that need safe cleanup, and activity in an attic or wall, especially as nights cool. We also hear from people asking about exclusion work, sealing gaps before a problem starts rather than after.

Because the borough includes homes and small commercial buildings close together, we also take calls from stores and offices asking about ongoing service rather than a one-time visit. Our commercial rodent control page explains how that differs from a single-visit home call, and our mouse control page covers the most common residential situation.

How a visit works here.

We inspect first, checking the interior and exterior for droppings, gnaw marks and entry gaps. Then we treat active rodents with traps and, where it fits the property, tamper-resistant stations kept away from children and pets.

After treatment, we seal the gaps we found with materials rodents cannot chew through, not steel wool alone, and we schedule a follow-up to confirm the work held. If damage needs repair, such as chewed insulation or baseboards, that is a separate estimate under our rodent damage repair service.

Recurring plans and seasonal timing.

Some Freehold callers ask about a recurring or yearly plan rather than a single visit, especially for commercial buildings or homes that have had repeat activity. We can talk through what a recurring schedule looks like on the call.

Fall is generally the busiest season for indoor rodent calls across our coverage area, as cooling nights push mice toward buildings. If your activity started recently and the weather just turned, that timing lines up with a normal seasonal pattern.

Can I set up a recurring pest plan instead of a single visit?

Yes. Some callers, especially commercial properties, prefer a recurring schedule. Tell us on the call and we can talk through what that looks like.

How do I know if it is mice or rats near my foundation?

Rats tend to burrow outside near foundations, sheds and decks, while mice are more often found indoors in attics and pantries. Droppings size is a fast clue: mouse droppings are small and pointed, rat droppings are larger and blunt.

Is bait safe if I have young kids at home?

We use tamper-resistant stations kept out of reach of children and pets, and we avoid loose bait. Tell us about kids in the home so we plan placement around them.
