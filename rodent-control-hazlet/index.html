How a Hazlet visit works.

Every visit starts the same way, no matter which town we're in. We walk the property, inside and out, and look for where rodents are getting in, not just where you last saw one.

Once we know the entry points and, where we can tell, the species, we treat what is active with traps and tamper-resistant stations placed on real travel paths. We then close the gaps with materials that hold up to chewing, such as hardware cloth, sheet metal or mortar, and set a follow-up to confirm the activity has stopped.

If entry points are the main issue rather than a heavy active problem, our rodent exclusion service can be scheduled on its own after an inspection identifies the gaps.

What Hazlet residents call us about.

Mice versus rats, quickly.

Mouse droppings run about an eighth to a quarter inch and taper to a point. Rat droppings are larger, roughly a half to three-quarters of an inch, and blunt at the ends. The animal you're dealing with changes where we place traps and what we seal first.

Rodents are also linked to health concerns, including salmonellosis and other illnesses spread through contact with droppings or contaminated food, according to the CDC. If anyone in the home has symptoms after cleanup, it is worth mentioning to a physician.

Chewed wiring is a separate but related concern. Damaged insulation on electrical wiring can contribute to fire risk, which is part of why we treat gnaw marks on wiring as a priority during an inspection rather than a minor detail.

White-footed mice sometimes show up near wooded lots and heavy landscaping in this part of Monmouth County, in addition to the common house mouse. They tend to favor sheds, garages and porches over the inside of a house, so we check outbuildings along with the main structure.

Before we arrive.

  • Keep pets and children away from the area with activity.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings.
  • Do not set out loose bait before the visit.
  • Note where and when you noticed activity.
  • Clear access to the attic hatch or garage storage if you can.
  • If a car is involved, park it somewhere we can check it easily.
