A township with both homes and businesses.

Holmdel is an incorporated township in Monmouth County, with its own local government. Unlike a few of the towns we cover, it is not an unincorporated section of a neighboring municipality. We still don't keep an office here; our base stays in Little Silver, and we travel out to handle calls.

Because Holmdel has a mix of residential streets and commercial buildings, we take calls from both. If you manage a store or office space, our commercial rodent control page explains how those visits differ from a typical house call.

What people usually call about.

Rodents in an attic or wall void account for a large share of calls, usually described as scratching at night or a scurrying sound behind a wall. Droppings cleanup is another common request, whether in a kitchen, garage, or basement.

We also hear from people asking about recurring or yearly pest plans rather than a single visit, especially for larger properties or homes that have had repeat activity. And a fair number of calls involve a duct or vent smell that turns out to be a dead rodent, which we cover in more detail on our dead rodent removal and odor control page.

How a Holmdel visit works.

The plan starts with an inspection, not a guess. A technician checks likely entry points outside, then follows droppings, grease marks, or sounds indoors to find where rodents are active.

  • Inspect the exterior foundation, roofline, and utility line penetrations.
  • Check the attic, garage, or crawl space for droppings and nesting material.
  • Place traps or tamper-resistant stations where the inspection points.
  • Seal confirmed entry points and schedule a follow-up to check results.

Larger properties and multi-building sites.

Holmdel includes some larger lots with a main house plus a garage, shed, or outbuilding, and rodent activity in one structure does not always mean the others are clear. A full inspection covers every building on the property, not just the one where you noticed a problem, since rodents move between them looking for food and shelter.

When a property has more than one structure, we walk each one during the same visit and note separately where sealing is needed, so nothing gets missed because it was outside the main house.

If a garage or basement connects directly to living space, that connection is worth mentioning on the call, since it can be the route rodents use to reach the rest of the house. Our garage and basement rodent control page covers how we handle that kind of layout.

Whether the property is a single home or a small campus of buildings, the goal stays the same: find where the rodents are getting in, remove what's already inside, and close the gap so the fix actually lasts. We'll walk you through exactly what the inspection found before any work starts, so you understand why we're placing traps or sealing in those specific spots.

Before we arrive.

  • Keep pets and children away from any area with droppings or a suspected nest.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings; ventilate the room and wear gloves if you must be in there.
  • Do not put out loose bait or poison before we arrive.
  • Note where and when you noticed activity, even roughly.
  • Clear access to the attic, garage, or crawl space if you can do so safely.

What does a duct or vent smell usually mean?

It often means a dead rodent decomposing near the ductwork. We locate the source, remove it, and treat the area rather than just masking the odor.

Is treatment safe with pets in the house?

Tell us about your pets when you call. We plan trap and station placement to limit their access to treated areas.

How is a rat problem different from a mouse problem here?

Rats tend to leave larger droppings and gnaw marks, and often nest outdoors near foundations. We confirm the species during inspection so the plan matches what's actually there.
