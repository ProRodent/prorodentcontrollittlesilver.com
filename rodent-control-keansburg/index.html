Sealing, damage, and why they go together.

A rodent that got in through a gap usually left some evidence of the trip. That can be gnawed wood around the gap itself, chewed insulation nearby, or a stained trail along a beam where the animal traveled repeatedly. We look for that kind of damage during the inspection, not just for the rodents themselves.

When we find damage, we can estimate the repair as part of the same visit through our rodent damage repair page, rather than sending you to find a separate contractor. Common repair items include chewed wiring covers, torn duct insulation, and gnawed trim or baseboards near an entry point.

Sealing itself uses materials chosen for durability against gnawing, not just for a tight fit. Hardware cloth, sheet metal flashing, copper mesh packed into small gaps, and mortar for masonry gaps are all common choices depending on the location. Foam alone does not hold up, since rodents can chew through it in a short time.

Attics, crawl spaces, and cleanup after activity clears.

Once rodents are removed and the entry points are sealed, some homes still need a cleanup pass. Old nesting material, scattered droppings, and disturbed insulation can remain in an attic or crawl space long after the activity itself has stopped.

That cleanup matters for more than appearance. Leftover droppings and nesting material can carry odor and, in some cases, attract new activity if they are not cleared out. Our attic and crawl space rodent cleanup service handles that step separately from the trapping and sealing work, so the space is actually clear when the job closes.

We'll walk you through which of these steps your property actually needs during the visit, rather than applying the same package to every call regardless of what the inspection turns up.

Before we arrive.

  • Keep pets and children away from any area with droppings or a suspected nest.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings.
  • Do not set out loose bait or poison before we arrive.
  • Write down where and when you noticed activity.
  • Clear access to the attic, basement, or garage if it's safe to do so.
