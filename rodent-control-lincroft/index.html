A section of Middletown Township, not a separate town.

Lincroft does not have its own borough hall, mayor or municipal government. It is an unincorporated section of Middletown Township, and residents here fall under Middletown's local services. We mention that up front because we would rather be accurate than vague about how the area is organized.

We do not keep an office in Lincroft. We are a Little Silver company, and Lincroft's 07738 ZIP falls inside the area we regularly serve, about 4 miles from our base.

How a visit here is scoped and run.

We start with an inspection, inside the home and around the outside, looking for droppings, gnaw marks and the gaps rodents are using to get in. That inspection is what sets the rest of the plan, and it is the same first step described on our rodent inspection and estimate page.

After the inspection, we treat what we find using traps and, where the property allows it, tamper-resistant stations placed along active travel routes. Once trapping is underway, we move to sealing the entry points, since leaving gaps open lets new rodents in even after the current ones are gone.

A follow-up visit confirms traps are clear and activity has stopped. We do not consider a job finished until that check is done.

Calls from unincorporated sections like Lincroft cover the same range we see anywhere in our coverage area. General mice or rat activity is the most common reason people reach out, often alongside a request to clean up droppings once they realize what they are looking at.

We also hear from people who want help sealing a house after repeated mouse activity, which is its own service on our rodent exclusion page, and from people asking about a recurring pest plan rather than a single visit. Attic and wall activity, especially scratching at night, is another frequent reason for a call.

Inland houses and older construction.

Away from the immediate waterfront, houses in this part of Monmouth County still see plenty of rodent activity, especially as temperatures drop in fall and rodents look for a warm place to nest. Gaps around dryer vents, utility penetrations and garage door tracks are common entry points across a wide range of home ages, not just older construction.

We treat garages, basements and crawl spaces as part of the same inspection as the main living area, since rodents often move between them freely. Our garage and basement rodent control and attic and crawl space rodent cleanup pages describe how those specific areas are handled once activity is confirmed.

Why we do not skip the follow-up.

A visit is not finished the day traps go down. We return to check what was caught, remove spent traps, and look for any new activity before we call a job complete. Skipping that step is how a problem that looked solved comes back a few weeks later.

Before we arrive.

  • Keep pets and kids away from any spot where you have seen droppings or activity.
  • Note where and when you heard or saw signs of rodents.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings.
  • Do not put out loose bait on your own before we arrive.
  • Clear access to the attic, crawl space or garage if that is where activity is concentrated.
