How a rodent visit works.

The process is the same everywhere we work. We inspect first, both inside and outside, to find droppings, gnaw marks and the gaps rodents are using. Then we treat active rodents with traps and, where appropriate, tamper-resistant stations kept away from children and pets.

After treatment, we seal the entry points we found with materials rodents cannot chew through, such as hardware cloth and sheet metal, not steel wool alone. Larger jobs with several entry points fall under our rodent exclusion service. We follow up to confirm traps and stations are no longer catching. If chewing has caused damage, our rodent damage repair service can be part of the plan.

Cooler months and attic activity.

As nights cool in fall, mice look for warm places to nest, and attics, garages and wall voids are common targets across Monmouth County, Marlboro included. Scratching that starts around dusk and continues into the night is a frequent early sign.

If you hear activity like that, note the time it starts and roughly where in the house it is loudest. That detail helps us plan the inspection route before we ever set a trap.

Homes, garages and outbuildings.

A township the size of Marlboro includes a wide mix of property types, from single-family homes on larger lots to attached garages, sheds and detached outbuildings. Each of those spaces gets checked a little differently, since a garage door gap behaves differently than a gap around a dryer vent.

If you store firewood, birdseed or pet food in a garage or shed, that can draw rodents looking for an easy meal near the house. Our garage and basement rodent control service covers those connected spaces as part of a full inspection, not just the living areas.

Have this ready when you call.

  • The room or area where you have heard or seen activity.
  • Roughly when it started and whether it happens at a certain time of day.
  • Whether pets or children spend time in that part of the house.
  • Whether you have found droppings, chewed items or a smell.

Before we arrive.

  • Keep pets and children away from the area with activity.
  • Note the room and approximate time you hear or see rodents.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings.
  • Do not set out loose bait before the visit.
  • Clear a path to the attic, garage or crawl space if you can.
