How the visit itself works.

We inspect first, walking the exterior and interior to find entry points near rooflines, vents, foundations and utility lines. Then we treat active rodents with traps and tamper-resistant stations placed along real travel paths, not scattered at random.

After that, we seal the gaps with materials that hold up to chewing and schedule a follow-up to confirm the work held. For a store or office, our commercial rodent control service follows the same steps, just timed around opening hours and storage areas.

Trapping without sealing rarely lasts.

A few store-bought traps can catch the mice you see today, but they do not close the gap that let them in. New rodents pick up the same scent trail and move in through the same opening within weeks.

That is why we pair trapping with sealing rather than offering one without the other. A rodent trapping and removal visit handles the active animals, and the sealing step is what actually keeps the property clear afterward.

Materials matter too. Steel wool alone breaks down and gets pushed out, so we rely on hardware cloth, sheet metal, copper mesh and mortar depending on the gap, along with door sweeps and chimney caps where those are the weak points.

In New Jersey, commercial pesticide applicators must be licensed through the NJDEP Pesticide Control Program, and it is reasonable to ask any provider for that license number before work begins on a Matawan property.

Mice breed year-round indoors, with litters roughly every three weeks, so a small problem noticed early is worth acting on before it grows through the winter.

Before we arrive.

  • Keep pets and children away from the area with activity.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings.
  • Do not set out loose bait on your own before the visit.
  • Note where and when you saw or heard rodents.
  • Clear access to the attic, basement or garage if you can.
