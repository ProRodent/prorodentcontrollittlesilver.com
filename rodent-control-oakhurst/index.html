What we handle for Oakhurst callers.

General mice and rat activity makes up most of the calls we get from this part of Monmouth County. Right behind that is droppings cleanup, where someone has found evidence but has not seen the animal itself, and wants to know it is handled safely rather than swept up loose.

How the visit itself works.

Every visit starts with looking, not setting traps blind. A technician checks for droppings, gnaw marks, grease trails and gaps around utility lines, garage doors and foundations, since those are common entry points in this part of the state.

From there, treatment and sealing happen together. We place traps or stations along the routes we find rodents using, then close the gaps that let them in with materials they cannot chew through. For a closer look at how we scope a job before work begins, see our rodent inspection and estimate page. A return visit confirms the traps are clear and nothing new has moved in.

If the concern is specifically a dead animal and the smell it leaves behind, that is a distinct job with its own process, covered on our dead rodent removal and odor control page.

Checklist before we arrive.

A little prep on your end helps the visit go faster. Keep the area where you saw activity clear if you can, and try to note the times of day you have heard or seen anything, since that helps us judge where to focus first.

Garages, sheds and outdoor structures.

Detached garages and sheds often get overlooked until activity is well established, since they are visited less often than the main house. Stored boxes, firewood and bags of birdseed or pet food give rodents both cover and a food source, and we check these spots as part of a full inspection, not as an afterthought.

Outdoor activity around a yard, deck or foundation line is its own category of call, and our backyard and outdoor rodent control page covers how we approach burrows and outdoor nesting separately from indoor work.

Commercial calls in the area.

Stores and small businesses near this stretch of Monmouth County also call us, usually about activity noticed in a stockroom or near a kitchen line. Our commercial rodent control service works around business hours and food-handling rules, so a visit does not disrupt the workday any more than it has to.

If the problem has been going on for a while.

Some calls come in after weeks or months of noticing small signs and hoping they would stop. That is common, and it does not change how we approach the visit, though a longer-running problem sometimes means more entry points to find and seal. Tell us honestly how long you think it has been happening so we can plan the inspection accordingly, and we will size the visit to match rather than guessing from the phone call alone.

Before we arrive.

  • Keep pets and children away from areas with droppings or signs of activity.
  • Write down where and when you noticed rodents, even rough times help.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings before we look at them.
  • Skip any loose bait or poison on your own.
  • Clear access to the garage, attic or crawl space if that is where the activity is.
