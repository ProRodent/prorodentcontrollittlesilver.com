Ocean Grove is part of Neptune Township.

Ocean Grove does not have its own town government. It is an unincorporated section of Neptune Township, without a separate borough hall or mayor of its own. That doesn't change how we cover it: the 07756 ZIP is on our list, and calls from here get handled the same as any other town we serve.

We are based in Little Silver, not in Ocean Grove, so there is no local office here. What we provide is a phone line that covers this ZIP and technicians who come out to handle the visit.

A tightly packed shore community, and what that means for rodents.

Ocean Grove has closely spaced, older homes near the water, which is common in shore towns generally. Buildings that age and sit close together can share more wall voids and roof lines than newer, more spread-out construction, which can make it easier for rodent activity to move between structures. We don't know the specifics of your building, so we confirm entry points during the inspection rather than assuming.

What people call about.

Attic and wall activity is a frequent reason for calls here, often described as scratching at night or scurrying between rooms. Dead animal odor is another common one, especially in homes with older ductwork where a dead rodent can be hard to locate without help.

We also hear from people managing a rental or vacation property who want a pre-move-out cleanup done before new tenants arrive. If that's your situation, our attic and crawl space rodent cleanup page covers that kind of visit, and our dead rodent removal and odor control page covers odor specifically.

How the visit is structured.

We inspect first, then treat, then seal, then follow up to confirm the work held. Sealing is often the step that matters most in older, closely built homes, since a single gap can serve more than one unit.

  • Inspect the exterior, attic, and any shared wall lines for entry points.
  • Place traps or stations where the inspection shows activity.
  • Seal confirmed gaps with materials rodents can't chew through.
  • Return to check results before the work is considered closed.

What we need from you on the call.

Tell us your ZIP code and roughly where in the property the activity is concentrated. If you're calling on behalf of a rental or a family member's home, real estate agents and property managers reach out to us fairly often, let us know that too, since it can change who we coordinate the visit with.

We'll also ask whether you'd prefer a recurring plan or a single visit. Seasonal or vacation properties sometimes see activity build up between visits when nobody is checking the house regularly, and a recurring plan can catch that before it turns into a bigger job.

Emergency situations.

Most calls can wait for the next scheduled opening, but a few situations warrant faster attention: active chewing on wiring, a nest close to a heat source, or activity that has a resident unable to safely use part of the home. Say so plainly when you call the 24/7 line, and we'll flag it and move it ahead of the regular schedule.

Outside of true emergencies, most visits are scheduled within the normal call window, and we'll give you a realistic sense of timing based on the current schedule rather than a vague promise. That applies to full-time residents and to owners who manage the property from somewhere else.

Before we arrive.

  • Keep pets and children away from any area with droppings or a suspected nest.
  • Do not sweep or vacuum dry droppings.
  • Do not set out loose bait or poison on your own before we arrive.
  • Note where and when you noticed activity, even roughly.
  • Clear access to the attic or crawl space if you can do so safely.
