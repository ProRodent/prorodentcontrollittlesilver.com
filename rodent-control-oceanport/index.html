What we handle in Oceanport.

Rodent calls from this area cover a wide range. Some people notice droppings in a kitchen drawer or garage shelf. Others hear scratching in an attic or wall at night and want to know what is up there before it gets worse. A dead-animal smell that will not clear is another common reason to call, since it usually means an animal died somewhere inside a wall or duct.

We also handle rodents that get into a parked vehicle, chewing wiring or building a nest in the engine bay, which is more common in cooler months. If mice and ants showed up together, or if a store or office wants a look at a stockroom, those calls come in too, and we treat each one with the right service rather than a one-size approach.

We keep the same standard for every call, whether it is a single mouse in a kitchen or a larger rat problem near a foundation. The steps do not change based on how big the job looks over the phone.

Our process here.

We start with an inspection to find where rodents are entering and what is active, checking the roofline, foundation, vents and any spot with signs of activity. From there we treat with traps and tamper-resistant stations rather than loose bait scattered around.

After the active problem is under control, we seal the openings we found so new rodents cannot use the same route in. A follow-up visit checks that the fix held, since trapping without sealing tends to become a repeating job.

A handful of calls involve mixed activity, mice and ants together, usually near a kitchen or a torn screen. We treat both when needed, rather than sending you to figure out which pest is which on your own.

Why sealing matters as much as trapping.

Traps remove the rodents that are already inside, but they do nothing about the gap that let them in. Left open, that same gap lets the next mouse or rat in a few weeks later, which is why a visit that only sets traps often turns into a repeat call.

Sealing uses materials rodents cannot chew through, such as hardware cloth, sheet metal and mortar, not steel wool alone. On older homes near the water, common spots include gaps around utility lines, vents and where a deck or porch meets the foundation.

Before you call.

You do not need a full diagnosis before dialing. A short description, like scratching above the garage at night, gives us enough to start. Mention pets or kids in the home and roughly how long the activity has been going on.

If you are calling about a business, let us know your operating hours so we can plan the visit around them instead of during your busiest time.

Before we arrive.

  • Keep pets and children away from any area with signs of activity.
  • Note where and when you saw or heard rodents.
  • Do not sweep dry droppings until we advise on safe cleanup.
  • Do not set out loose bait on your own.
  • Clear access to the attic, crawl space or garage if it is safe to do so.

How can I tell if it is mice or rats?

Mouse droppings are small and pointed, around 1/8 to 1/4 inch. Rat droppings are larger and blunt, closer to 1/2 to 3/4 inch. We confirm during the visit rather than guessing from a distance.

Do you check garages and vehicles as well as houses?

Yes. Garages, basements and parked vehicles are common spots for rodent activity, and our garage and basement and vehicle rodent removal services cover those separately from the main house.

Is treatment safe with pets in the house?

We avoid loose bait and place traps and tamper-resistant stations out of reach. Tell us about pets or children so placement matches the household.
