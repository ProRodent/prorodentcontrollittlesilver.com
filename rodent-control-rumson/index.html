We take calls on behalf of homeowners directly, and also from property managers and real estate agents handling a listing or a transition between tenants. The process is the same either way, and we coordinate access with whoever is available.

Signs that bring people to call.

Most calls start with something specific: scratching heard above a bedroom ceiling at night, droppings found while cleaning out a pantry shelf, or a nest with a mother and young discovered somewhere unexpected, like a shed or an outbuilding. A smell that keeps coming back, even after cleaning, often points to a dead animal inside a wall or duct.

Others call after finding chewed insulation or wiring in a parked car, which happens more in colder months when engine bays stay warm. Some calls are about ongoing prevention rather than an active problem, like wanting a yearly plan in place before the next season.

Whatever brought you to call, the visit follows the same order every time: look first, treat what is active, close the gaps, then confirm the fix worked. We do not skip steps to save time on a smaller-looking job.

Coastal Monmouth County and rodent activity.

Coastal towns along this part of the Jersey Shore often mix older housing stock built closer to the water with newer construction set further back. Older homes tend to have more small gaps at sills, vents and utility lines, which is exactly what a mouse looks for, since a gap of about 1/4 inch is enough.

We do not have specific records on individual Rumson properties, so we base the visit on what we actually find during the inspection, not on assumptions about the neighborhood.

A few calls involve mixed activity, such as mice and ants appearing around the same window or door. We address both where it makes sense during the same visit rather than treating them as unrelated problems.

How we approach a visit.

We inspect the exterior and interior first, checking the roofline, foundation and any area with signs of activity. We treat with traps and tamper-resistant stations, never loose bait, and then seal the gaps we found so the fix holds after we leave.

A follow-up visit confirms the activity has actually stopped. If it has not, we look again for a missed entry point rather than just adding more traps.

Vehicles, garages and outbuildings.

A rodent problem is not always inside the main house. Detached garages, sheds and parked vehicles can all host a nest, especially where there is stored cardboard, firewood or insulation to burrow into. Our garage and basement rodent control service covers those spaces the same way we cover living areas.

If a car sits unused for stretches at a time, check under the hood before starting it, since rodents sometimes chew belts and wiring while nesting near a warm engine. A quick look can save a repair bill later.

If you are unsure whether what you are hearing is rodents or something else, describe the sound and timing anyway. We can usually tell from a short description whether it fits a rodent pattern or points somewhere else.

What to mention on the call.

Let us know if your property is close to water, since that can affect where rodents travel from. Mention pets or children in the household too, so we place traps and stations accordingly.

If you found the activity in an outbuilding rather than the house itself, say so, since that changes where we start the inspection.

Before we arrive.

  • Keep pets and children away from any area with signs of activity.
  • Note where and when you saw or heard rodents, including any nest.
  • Do not sweep dry droppings. Wait for guidance on safe cleanup.
  • Do not put out loose bait or poison before we arrive.
  • Clear access to the attic, shed or garage if you can do so safely.
