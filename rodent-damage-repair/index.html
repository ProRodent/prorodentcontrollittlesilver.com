Stop the rodents first, then fix what they broke.

Repairs done while rodents are still active get chewed again. New insulation gets shredded for nests, and a patched soffit gets a new hole beside the old one. So the order matters: find the entry points, remove the animals, close the gaps, and only then repair the damage.

If you are not sure whether the problem is active, start with rodent inspection. It tells us what is still going on and which damage is old, so you do not pay to repair the same spot twice. We also note what looks recent and what looks long settled, which helps if a contractor asks about the history.

What rodents do to an attic and its ducts.

In many older homes, attics are low and tight, with insulation laid between joists and ducts running through the same space. Rodents nest in that insulation, tunnel through it and tear at the duct wrap.

Damaged duct insulation and flex duct can leak conditioned air into the attic, and soiled insulation may need replacing. If you notice uneven heating or cooling in an upstairs room, mention it when you call. It can point to a chewed or crushed duct.

Wiring: a fire risk that goes to an electrician.

Rodents chew wire insulation, and exposed or damaged wiring can contribute to a fire. This is the one repair we do not touch. In New Jersey, electrical repairs must be done by a licensed electrical contractor, so we flag any chewed wiring we find and tell you which areas to have checked, so the electrician knows where to start.

If you see gnawed cable, flickering lights, a burning smell or a tripped breaker that will not stay reset, turn off the affected circuit if you can do so safely and call an electrician right away. Then call us so the rodents are dealt with at the same time.

We flag chewed wiring for the right trade. We never repair it ourselves.

Openings at the roofline and pipes.

Rodents find the same weak points again and again: a gap where fascia meets soffit, a loose vent screen, or an opening around a pipe. Mice need only about a quarter inch, and rats about half an inch. A gnawed edge can grow into a doorway. Photos of each opening before sealing give you a record of the work.

We close openings with materials rodents cannot chew, such as hardware cloth, sheet metal and mortar, and we explain the full approach in rodent exclusion. Foam or steel wool alone gets chewed out or pulled away, so we do not rely on them.

Pipes, drywall and the damage you find later.

Rodents gnaw on plastic supply lines and drain pipes, usually where they run through a wall or under a sink. A small chew can leak slowly for weeks, so look for stains, swelling cabinet bases and a musty smell. If you find one, shut off the water to that fixture and call a plumber.

Drywall damage is often cosmetic: ragged holes at baseboards, gnawed corners and torn edges around pipes. It is also a map. A hole shows where they travel, and we use it to trace runs back to the entry point. Patch the wall only after that route is sealed, or the patch just moves the problem along.

Does insurance pay for rodent damage?

Often it does not. Standard homeowners policies commonly exclude damage caused by rodents and vermin. Look for the vermin or pest exclusion in your policy, or ask your agent, before you plan around a claim, and ask whether any related loss, such as damage from a fire, is treated differently.

We can describe what we found in plain terms, and you can pass that on. We do not tell you what your policy covers, since that is between you and your insurer. Keep photos of damage before anything is removed, and note the date you first saw it.

Document the damage first.

  • Take photos of gnawed wiring, ducts, pipes and framing before anything is moved.
  • Do not touch exposed wire, and keep the circuit off if you turned it off.
  • Note the rooms with uneven heating or cooling, or a musty odor.
  • Do not patch openings with foam or steel wool alone, because rodents chew through both.

Questions about rodent damage repair

Why can't you repair wiring?

In New Jersey, electrical repairs must be done by a licensed electrical contractor. We flag chewed wiring and tell you where to have it checked, then deal with the rodents so the damage does not return.

Should I repair the damage before the rodents are gone?

No. Fresh materials get chewed and nested in again. Stop the rodents, seal the entry points and confirm activity has stopped, then repair.

Will my homeowners insurance cover rodent damage?

Often not, since standard policies commonly exclude damage by rodents and vermin. Your agent can confirm what your own policy says.

Can chewed ducts really affect my heating and cooling?

They can. Torn duct wrap or a punctured flex duct can leak air into the attic, so an upstairs room may run hot or cold.

Do you replace attic insulation?

We handle contaminated insulation as part of cleanup work. Larger replacement jobs may involve another contractor, and we tell you when that applies.

Can I call at night if I find gnawed wires or a leak?

Call an electrician or plumber first for the immediate hazard. Our 24/7 line covers the rodent side, and a same-day visit depends on the schedule.
