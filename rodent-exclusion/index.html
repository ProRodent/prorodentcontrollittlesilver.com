A house only stays clear once the holes are closed.

Trapping removes the animals you have. Exclusion keeps the next ones from moving in. A mouse needs a gap of only about 1/4 inch, roughly the width of a pencil, and a rat needs about 1/2 inch. Most homes have dozens of openings that size.

Sealing is the last step in a sequence, not the first. If we close every hole while animals are still inside, they chew new ones or die in the walls. That is why we start with rodent trapping and removal and seal only after the traps go quiet.

Little Silver houses and where they leak.

Many Little Silver houses have gained a rear addition or dormer over the years. The seam where the new roofline meets the old wall, and where the new slab meets the old foundation, is often sealed less carefully than the rest, so we check those joints first.

Older foundations bring their own gaps: cracked mortar joints, stone or block that has shifted, and old pipe sleeves that no longer fit the pipe. Stand outside and look at the bottom two feet of each wall. Any dark rub mark near a gap, or a smooth worn edge, is a sign something is using it. Take a photo of anything suspicious so we can find it again.

The usual entry points we check.

  • Chimney tops without a cap, and the gap around the flashing.
  • Dryer, bath and range vents with a missing or bent flap.
  • Soffit and fascia joints, where a roof edge has pulled away from the wall.
  • Utility penetrations for gas, electric, cable and water lines, often left open around the pipe.
  • Garage door corners, worn bottom seals and gaps under exterior doors.
  • Foundation vents, sill plates and gaps where an addition meets the original house.

Materials by gap size.

The right fix depends on the size of the hole and what it is in. Foam sprayed alone is chewed through quickly, and so is plastic. A rodent cannot chew through metal or hard masonry, so those are the base of every repair.

Why steel wool alone does not hold.

Steel wool is the tip most people hear first. It rusts in damp air and crumbles, and a determined rodent pulls it out strand by strand and uses it in the nest. Packed into a hole and left alone, it fails within a season or two.

Copper mesh does not rust the same way, and it works when it is packed tightly and covered with sealant or mortar. Foam alone has the same weakness as steel wool. It is fine as a filler behind metal, but never as the only barrier.

Rodents chew foam and pull out steel wool. They do not get through metal.

How the work is sequenced on site.

A technician walks the whole exterior and, where there is safe access, the roof edge and the attic or crawl space. Each opening is marked and photographed for the plan. The biggest and highest-risk holes are sealed first, with one small gap left near active traps until the catch stops.

After exclusion, droppings and nesting material still need to be dealt with. If the noise was in the roofline, attic and crawl space rodent cleanup is the step that follows. If the damage includes chewed siding, vents or wiring, rodent damage repair covers the repair side.

Sealing done in cold weather needs a little care. Some sealants do not cure below their rated temperature, so in a December cold snap we lean on metal and mortar that hold up in the cold, and finish the sealant work on a milder day. Nothing about a rodent problem gets better by waiting, so we still close the largest holes first.

What you can do before we arrive.

  • Walk the outside of the house and note any gap wider than a pencil, especially at pipes and vents.
  • Trim shrubs and vines that touch the siding, since they give rodents a ramp to the soffit.
  • Check the dryer vent flap and make sure it closes when the dryer is off.
  • Do not fill holes with foam or paper yourself, because you may seal an animal in.
  • Tell us where you hear the noise, and at what time of night.

Questions about rodent exclusion and entry point sealing

Can I just seal the holes myself with steel wool and caulk?

That works for a single small gap, but rodents chew caulk and drag out steel wool, so it tends to fail. Metal mesh or flashing with sealant over it holds far longer. Sealing before the animals are out can also trap them inside.

How do you find all the entry points?

We start outside and work from the foundation up to the roofline, then look inside for rub marks, droppings and daylight around pipes. Mice leave grease marks at tight gaps, and those show us which ones are in use.

Will sealing damage my siding or chimney?

We use screws and flashing that sit flush against the siding, and hardware cloth cut to fit. Chimney caps clamp to the flue without changing the brickwork. We tell you what we plan to do to each spot before we do it.

Do I need to seal even if I have not seen a rodent yet?

Sealing is a good idea if you have seen gnaw marks, droppings or a pest report from a neighbor. Older foundations and additions often have gaps that have not been used yet.

Do you take calls late in the evening or on holidays?

Yes, someone answers at any hour, including holidays, and a same-day look is offered when the calendar has room. An open hole in the roofline during a cold snap is worth a call.

How long before rodents stop coming in after exclusion?

Once every used opening is sealed and the animals inside are removed, new entry should stop. We recheck the work, since a rodent that cannot get in will test new spots near the old one.
