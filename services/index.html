Start with the animal if you know it. Mouse control fits small pointed droppings, scratching in walls and pantry raids. Rat control fits larger droppings, burrows and gnawing near foundations, sheds and water. If you are not sure which you have, a rodent inspection identifies the species and the entry points.

Next, match the place. Noise overhead usually means attic and crawl space cleanup is needed after the animals are gone. Garages and cellars, including damp ones near the river, fall under garage and basement rodent control. Burrows, sheds and decks belong to backyard and outdoor rodent control.

Some problems are about the aftermath. A smell in a wall points to dead rodent removal and odor control. Chewed wiring or insulation may need rodent damage repair once the animals are out, though electrical repairs are for licensed electricians. Chewed hoses and nests under a hood call for vehicle and car rodent removal.

Two services cut across everything else. Rodent trapping and removal deals with the animals that are there now. Rodent exclusion closes the gaps so new ones cannot follow. Shops, restaurants and offices can use commercial rodent control, and any active emergency goes straight to the 24/7 line.

Most homes need more than one service, and the order matters. The usual plan starts with inspection, because everything else depends on knowing the species and where they enter. Treatment follows, so that the animals inside are removed before any hole is closed.

Sealing comes only after that. If you close a gap while animals are still inside, they can chew a new way out or die in the wall. Once the house is sealed and quiet, cleanup and odor work can start, because disturbing droppings while animals are still active spreads the mess.

Repairs are last. Insulation, chewed trim and damaged vents get replaced only once rodent activity has stopped. Otherwise the new work gets chewed again. Follow-up visits sit between each stage and confirm that traps and stations are no longer catching anything.

In Little Silver, we also fit the plan to the site. Riverside and streamside homes often add outdoor work early, since rats burrow along banks. Mid-century interior homes usually start in the attic and eaves. Homes on low streets that flood at high tide add a garage and crawl space check after each event.

You do not need to plan this yourself. Call, describe what you noticed, and we will suggest the starting point and the likely order of work.

Questions about our services

Which service should I call about first?

Choose the one that matches your main symptom, such as noise, droppings or smell. If you are unsure, an inspection is the safest start.

Can I book cleanup without treatment?

Not usually. Cleaning while animals are still active means the mess returns. Treatment and sealing normally come first.

Do you cover addresses outside Little Silver?

Yes. We are based in Little Silver and also cover towns across Monmouth County within about 15 miles. See the areas we cover page for the full list and each town's ZIP code.

Is there a night line for emergencies?

Yes. Someone answers the line at every hour, and a same-day visit is offered when the day has room.

Do I need to prepare before a visit?

Note where you hear or see activity and keep pets and children away from it. Do not sweep dry droppings or set out loose bait.
